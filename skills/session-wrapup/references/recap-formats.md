# Recap Formats and Style Guide

Recaps remind players what happened and reinforce the campaign's
emotional texture.

## Format: Table Read (Narrative Prose)

The "Previously on..." read aloud before the next session: about
two minutes, ~300-400 words.

### Structure

1. **Anchor** (1-2 sentences) — where and when.
2. **Key events** (2-3 paragraphs) — organized by dramatic beats,
   not chronology; lead with the most emotionally resonant moment;
   highlight player decisions and their consequences.
3. **Cliffhanger** (1-2 sentences) — forward momentum: an open
   question, an approaching threat, or a decision that demands
   action.

Past tense for events, present for ongoing states.

### Tone Calibration

**Gothic horror / Call of Cthulhu:** atmospheric, foreboding;
emphasize what the investigators don't yet understand; sensory
detail (sound, smell, quality of light); end on dread, not
resolution.

> The gaslit streets of Vienna offered no warmth as the
> investigators made their way back from the opera. Behind
> them, the State Opera's grand facade still blazed with
> candlelight — but the performance they had witnessed
> backstage would not leave their minds so easily.

**Regency social drama:** formal but warm, period diction; center
relationships, reputation shifts, alliances formed or broken; end
on a social complication or romantic tension.

**Action/adventure / D&D:** punchy, momentum-driven; lead with the
biggest beat; note combat outcomes and loot with energy; end on the
next challenge or destination.

**Intrigue / Blades in the Dark:** terse, noir; who betrayed whom,
what was stolen, who noticed; faction clock ticks as consequences;
end on what's owed and who's watching.

### Avoid

- Player names (use character names)
- Meta-game language ("you rolled a 97")
- Events listed without dramatic framing
- Information the PCs don't have
- More than 400 words

## Format: Quick Bullets

5-8 one-sentence bullets covering key events, decisions,
discoveries and what's unresolved; the last 1-2 bullets look
forward, e.g.:

- Lady Catherine accepted the Countess von Hagen's invitation
  to the diplomatic ball.
- **Next:** The ball is in three days. Hans is still at large.

## Entity Extraction Shorthand

When scanning play notes for new entities, categorize:

| Marker | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| NEW-NPC | Improvised character, needs vault entry |
| NEW-LOC | New location described, needs vault entry |
| NEW-ITEM | Item introduced, may need vault entry |
| NEW-EVENT | Significant event — create Event entity file and add linked entry to timeline. Must meet at least 2 of: changes entity state, multiple named participants, creates forward consequences, referenced from multiple entities. Minor events stay as inline timeline entries |
| UPDATE | Existing entity needs information update |
| CONFLICT | Contradicts existing vault content — flag |

## Campaign Journal Format

Only when the GM asks: a polished 800-1500-word write-up for a
blog, shared document or journal section. Third person, past
tense, omniscient narrator (may describe what PCs didn't see,
within reason); include memorable dialogue; `---` between major
beats; end on a hook for the next session.
